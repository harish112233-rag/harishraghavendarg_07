Great Expectations
By Charles Dickens

Chapter 1: The Convict on the Marshes

My father's family name being Pirrip, and my Christian name Philip, my infant tongue could make of both names nothing longer or more explicit than Pip. So I called myself Pip, and came to be called Pip. I give Pirrip as my father's family name on the authority of his tombstone and my sister, Mrs. Joe Gargery, who married the blacksmith.

On a cold, raw afternoon I found myself in the churchyard. A fearful man, all in coarse grey, with a great iron on his leg — a man who had been soaked in water, smothered in mud, lamed by stones, and torn by briars — started up from among the graves. He seized me by the chin and demanded food and a file. Terrified, I promised to bring both early the next morning. This encounter with the convict Abel Magwitch shaped the entire course of my life, though I did not know it then.

Chapter 2: Miss Havisham and Estella

Miss Havisham lived in Satis House, a grand decayed mansion. She had stopped all the clocks at twenty minutes to nine on her wedding day, when she was jilted by her lover. She still wore her wedding dress decades later, and the rotting wedding cake still sat on the table. She had adopted a beautiful girl named Estella and raised her to break men's hearts.

Estella was proud, refined, and scornful of my coarse hands and thick boots. Yet I fell desperately in love with her. Miss Havisham seemed to encourage my attachment, though her motives were darker than I understood. She had raised Estella as an instrument of revenge against all men.

Chapter 3: Great Expectations

One day I was visited by a lawyer, Mr. Jaggers, who informed me I had great expectations from an anonymous benefactor. I was to be educated as a gentleman in London. I assumed my benefactor was Miss Havisham and went to London full of hope and social ambition, turning away from my old friends Joe and Biddy in my shame of humble origins.

In London I lived beyond my means, fell into debt, and continued to pursue Estella who seemed incapable of returning love. She eventually married a brutal man named Drummle. My dreams of wealth and status began to crumble.

Chapter 4: The Truth About Magwitch

The convict Magwitch, now rich from years of hard work in Australia, returned secretly to England. He revealed himself as my true benefactor. The shock was profound. All my fine gentleman's education had been funded by a criminal. My dreams of Miss Havisham's approval and Estella's love collapsed. Yet as I came to know Magwitch better, I saw his genuine affection and loyalty, and felt a growing bond of human compassion despite the danger his presence brought.

Chapter 5: Redemption and Humility

Pip helped Magwitch attempt to escape England, but the plan failed. Magwitch was captured, convicted, and died in prison before he could be hanged. Pip fell ill from grief and debt. Joe came to London and nursed him back to health with quiet, uncomplaining love. Pip returned home humbled and grateful, understanding at last that true worth lay not in wealth or social standing but in loyalty, kindness, and honest work.
